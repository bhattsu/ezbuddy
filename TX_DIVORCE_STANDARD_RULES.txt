TEXAS DIVORCE FILING - STANDARD RULES

1. The divorce must be filed in the appropriate Texas county and court.

2. At least one spouse must meet the Texas residency requirements.

3. The person filing the divorce is the Petitioner. The other spouse is the Respondent.

4. An Original Petition for Divorce must be filed to start the divorce case.

5. The petition must provide basic information about the marriage, spouses, children (if any), property, debts, and the relief requested from the court.

6. After filing, the Respondent must receive proper legal notice unless service is legally waived.

7. If both spouses agree on all issues, the case may proceed as an uncontested/agreed divorce.

8. If the Respondent does not file an answer or otherwise respond within the required time after proper service, the Petitioner may be able to proceed by default.

9. A default divorce does not automatically mean the Petitioner receives everything requested. The court must still review the requested relief and supporting evidence.

10. If the spouses disagree about any material issue, the case may become contested and should not follow the standard uncontested workflow.

11. If there are children of the marriage, the divorce must address conservatorship (custody), possession and access (visitation), child support, and medical and dental support as applicable.

12. If there are no minor children of the marriage, the divorce should follow the applicable divorce-without-children requirements.

13. Property and debts must be identified and addressed as applicable.

14. Existing court orders involving the spouses or children must be identified because they may affect the forms and filing process.

15. Texas generally requires a minimum 60-day waiting period after the divorce petition is filed before the divorce can be finalized, subject to statutory exceptions.

16. Local court rules, standing orders, and county-specific filing requirements must also be followed.

17. A Final Decree of Divorce must be prepared before finalizing the case.

18. The required prove-up, hearing, affidavit, or other finalization procedure must be completed as permitted or required by the court.

19. The divorce is finalized when the judge signs the Final Decree of Divorce.

20. If the case involves disputed custody, complex property, family violence, jurisdiction problems, or other issues outside the standard workflow, additional legal review may be required.
